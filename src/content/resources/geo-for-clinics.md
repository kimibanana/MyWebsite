When a patient types “best dermatologist in Makati for acne scars” into ChatGPT, they don’t get ten blue links. They get a short paragraph that names two or three clinics and explains why. If your clinic isn’t one of those names, you don’t exist for that patient.

That shift is why we now plan every SEO engagement with **generative engine optimization (GEO)** in mind: the work of getting your practice cited and recommended by AI tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude and Google’s AI Overviews.

## Why AI search matters for healthcare

Patients have always researched before they book. What’s changed is where they do it. More of them start with an AI assistant because it feels like asking a knowledgeable friend: they can describe their symptoms, budget and location in plain language and get a tailored answer.

Two things make this especially important for clinics:

- **AI answers are short lists.** A Google results page shows ten organic results, a map pack and ads. An AI answer usually recommends a handful of providers. Being sixth is the same as being invisible.
- **AI-referred patients arrive pre-sold.** By the time someone clicks through from an AI answer, they’ve already been told why your clinic is a good fit. In our experience these visitors convert into bookings more readily than average search traffic.

GEO doesn’t replace Google. Most patients still check your Google reviews and Maps listing before they call. But the shortlist is increasingly being built inside AI tools first.

## How AI tools decide which clinics to cite

AI models don’t have a single ranking algorithm you can reverse-engineer. They draw on what they learned in training plus live web results, and they lean toward businesses that are **well-documented, consistent and trusted across many sources**. In practice that comes down to five things.

### 1. Your clinic is a clearly defined entity

AI tools need to be confident that “Bright Smile Dental Clinic, Quezon City” is one real business with a known address, services and doctors. Conflicting information creates doubt, and doubt keeps you out of answers.

- Use the exact same clinic name, address and phone number everywhere: your website, Google Business Profile, Facebook, directories and medical association listings.
- List each doctor’s full name, specialty, credentials and the clinics where they practice.
- Add structured data (schema) for `MedicalClinic`, `Physician` and `LocalBusiness` so machines can read these facts directly.

### 2. Your website answers real patient questions

AI tools love content they can quote. Vague marketing lines like “we provide world-class care” give them nothing to work with. Specific, factual answers do.

- Build a page for each core treatment that explains who it’s for, how it works, recovery time, typical price range and what to expect at the first visit.
- Add a short FAQ to each page using the exact questions patients ask your front desk.
- Keep facts current. Outdated prices, doctors who have left or old clinic hours erode trust quickly.

### 3. Reviews come from more than one place

Google reviews matter most for Maps, but AI tools read widely. Reviews on Facebook, healthcare directories and booking platforms (and, abroad, sites like Healthgrades, Doctify or RateMDs) all add to the picture.

- Ask happy patients for a review on Google first, then rotate in one or two other platforms that matter in your market.
- Reply to every review professionally, without confirming any medical details about the patient.

### 4. Other trusted sources mention you

Mentions in local news, medical society websites, university pages, health publications and podcasts tell AI tools that real people vouch for you.

- Pitch your doctors as expert sources to local media on seasonal health topics.
- Keep your medical society and hospital affiliation profiles complete and linked to your site.
- Sponsor or speak at community health events and make sure the organizer’s page names your clinic.

### 5. You show up across platforms

AI tools pull from video, social and forums as well as websites. A YouTube channel answering common treatment questions, an active Facebook page and an up-to-date LinkedIn profile for your doctors all reinforce who you are.

## Writing content that gets cited

Search used to reward pages built around a keyword. AI search rewards pages built around a **conversation**. Patients ask long, specific questions, so your content should answer them directly.

1. **Lead with the answer.** Put a clear, two-to-three-sentence answer at the top of each page or section, then expand.
2. **Be specific to your location.** “Laser hair removal in Cebu City: cost, sessions and what to expect” beats a generic national article.
3. **Show who wrote and reviewed it.** Add the reviewing doctor’s name, credentials and review date to every medical page.
4. **Use simple structure.** Clear headings, short paragraphs, lists and tables are easier for AI tools to parse and quote.
5. **Add FAQ schema.** Mark up your FAQs so search engines and AI tools can read the question-and-answer pairs directly.

## How to track your AI visibility

You can’t yet log in to a “ChatGPT Search Console”, but you can measure progress:

- **Referral traffic.** In Google Analytics, look for sessions from chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, gemini.google.com and similar sources.
- **Monthly prompt checks.** Each month, ask the main AI tools the questions your patients would ask, such as “best fertility clinic in Manila” or “where can I get Invisalign in Alabang”, and record whether you’re named.
- **Ask new patients.** Add “AI assistant (ChatGPT, Gemini, etc.)” as an option on your “How did you hear about us?” form.

## A 6-month GEO plan for clinics

| Months | Focus | Key tasks |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Foundation | Fix name, address and phone consistency; complete Google Business Profile; add clinic and doctor schema; set up tracking |
| 2–4 | Content | Rebuild core treatment pages; add FAQs; publish answers to your top 20 patient questions |
| 4–6 | Authority | Review program across 2–3 platforms; local media and association mentions; start a short-form video series |
| 6+ | Optimize | Review prompt checks and referral data; expand what’s working |

## Common mistakes

- **Treating GEO as a one-off project.** AI answers change as the web changes. Keep information fresh.
- **Publishing generic AI-written articles.** If your content reads like everyone else’s, there’s no reason to cite you.
- **Ignoring your Google Business Profile.** It’s one of the most widely referenced sources about any clinic.
- **Making claims you can’t support.** Health advertising rules apply to your website too. Avoid guarantees and unverifiable “best” claims.

## The bottom line

AI search is still young, which is exactly why it’s an opportunity. Clinics that become the clearly documented, well-reviewed, frequently mentioned option in their specialty and city now will be hard to displace later.
