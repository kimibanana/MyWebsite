For years, the advice was simple: publish more. More blog posts, more location pages, more keyword variations. Many clinic websites now have hundreds of pages, and most of them do nothing.

Today, that approach usually backfires. Google and AI tools reward websites that make it obvious **which page is the best answer for each treatment**. A smaller set of strong, specific pages almost always beats a large set of thin ones.

## The four page types your clinic actually needs

Every page on your website should have one clear job:

1. **Treatment or condition pages.** These win patients. Example: “Dental implants in Alabang”.
2. **Location pages.** One per real clinic branch, with address, hours, directions, doctors and services offered there.
3. **Doctor profiles.** Name, photo, specialty, credentials, training, languages and booking link.
4. **Supporting articles.** Answer one specific question and point readers to the related treatment page.

Most clinic websites have too many of types 2 and 4 and too few strong examples of type 1.

## Why publishing more stopped working

- **Duplicate pages compete with each other.** If you have three pages about “teeth whitening”, Google has to guess which one to rank, and often ranks none of them well. This is called keyword cannibalization.
- **City-swap pages look like spam.** Ten pages that are identical except for the city name don’t fool anyone.
- **Generic content has no reason to rank.** If a page says what every other clinic says, neither Google nor ChatGPT has a reason to choose it.
- **Health content is held to a higher standard.** Google treats medical information as “Your Money or Your Life” content and looks harder for real expertise.

## Step 1: Audit every page

1. Export a list of every URL on your site.
2. Pull at least 12 months of data from Google Search Console for each page: clicks, impressions and the queries it appears for.
3. Give each page one action: **keep, improve, merge, redirect or remove**.
4. To find duplicates, filter Search Console by a treatment query (for example “veneers”) and see how many of your pages get impressions for it.
5. Keep the strongest page for each topic and merge the useful parts of the others into it.
6. Redirect any page you remove that has backlinks or traffic, rather than deleting it outright.

## Step 2: Rebuild each treatment page around the patient’s question

A great treatment page answers what the patient searched for in the first paragraph, then proves you’re the right clinic.

- **Answer first.** “Dental implants at our Alabang clinic replace missing teeth with a titanium post and a natural-looking crown. Most patients need two to three visits over three to six months.”
- **Be local.** Mention the area, nearby landmarks, parking and transport. Use the “swap test”: if you could replace the city name and the page would still make sense, it isn’t local enough.
- **Prove expertise.** Name the doctors who perform the treatment, their training and experience, and real before-and-after cases where your market’s rules allow it.
- **Cover what patients worry about.** Cost range, pain, recovery, risks, financing and HMO or insurance coverage.
- **Stay compliant.** Avoid guarantees and “best in the country” claims. Have a doctor review every medical statement.

## Step 3: Support each priority page with a small cluster of articles

Pick your three to five most valuable treatments. For each, write a handful of articles that answer specific questions and link back to the treatment page. For dental implants, that might be:

- How much do dental implants cost in the Philippines?
- Dental implants vs dentures: which is right for you?
- Can you get implants if you have diabetes?
- How long do dental implants last?

Each article answers one question well and sends ready-to-book readers to the main page.

## Step 4: Link on purpose

- Link supporting articles up to their treatment page using descriptive anchor text, not “click here”.
- Link each location page to the treatments offered at that branch.
- Link doctor profiles to the treatments they perform, and vice versa.
- Update internal links to point to final URLs, not redirects.

## Supporting work that makes it stick

- **Schema** connecting your clinic, doctors and locations, with addresses that match your Google Business Profile exactly.
- **Technical cleanup** after merges: fix broken links, remove redirect chains and resubmit your sitemap.
- **Reviews**, which keep strong pages and Maps listings at the top.

## How to measure success

Total traffic is the wrong measure for this kind of project. Instead, track:

- Google Maps rankings across your service area, measured with a grid tool
- Visits to your priority treatment pages
- Calls, form submissions, WhatsApp messages and booked appointments

Expect this to take months, not weeks, and to need ongoing upkeep. Rankings earned this way are far more durable than a pile of thin pages.
