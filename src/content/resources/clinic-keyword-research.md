Good keyword research isn’t about finding the biggest numbers. A search like “acne” gets huge volume, but almost none of those people are ready to book. A search like “acne scar treatment Quezon City” gets a fraction of the volume, and many of those people are looking for a clinic today.

This guide walks through how we find the searches that turn into patients.

## The four types of clinic searches

| Type | Example | Intent | Best page type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Local treatment | “dental implants Alabang” | Ready to book | Treatment page |
| “Near me” | “dermatologist near me” | Ready to book | Google Business Profile + location page |
| Comparison and cost | “Invisalign vs braces cost Philippines” | Deciding | Supporting article |
| Symptom and question | “why do my gums bleed” | Researching | Supporting article |

Prioritize in that order. The first two bring patients fastest. The last two build trust and feed the first two.

## Step 1: Start with your business, not a tool

Before opening any keyword tool, list:

- The **treatments that make you the most money** or that you most want to grow
- The **conditions** patients come to you with
- The **areas** you realistically serve: city, districts and nearby towns
- The **questions** your front desk hears every week

That list keeps your research focused on patients you actually want.

## Step 2: Expand with real search data

- **Google autocomplete.** Type “[treatment] in [city]” and note the suggestions.
- **“People also ask”** boxes on Google results pages.
- **Google Search Console.** The queries you already get impressions for are your fastest wins.
- **Keyword tools** such as Google Keyword Planner, Ahrefs or Semrush for volume and competition.
- **Your own data.** Booking forms, WhatsApp chats and call notes show how patients describe their problems.

## Step 3: Group keywords by page, not by phrase

Don’t create a page for every variation. “Teeth whitening price”, “how much is teeth whitening” and “teeth whitening cost Makati” all belong on one teeth whitening page. Grouping prevents your pages from competing with each other.

A simple rule: **if Google shows mostly the same results for two searches, they belong on the same page.**

## Step 4: Judge intent by looking at the results

Search the keyword yourself and look at what ranks:

- **Map pack and clinic websites** → local, high intent. Build or improve a treatment page and your Google Business Profile.
- **Health encyclopedias and hospital guides** → informational. Write a helpful article that links to your treatment page.
- **Mostly marketplaces or directories** → you may need to be listed there as well as rank yourself.

## Step 5: Prioritize with a simple score

For each keyword group, score from 1 to 3:

- **Value:** how much is a patient for this treatment worth to you?
- **Intent:** how close is the searcher to booking?
- **Winnability:** how strong are the clinics currently ranking?

Start with groups that score high on value and intent, even if volume looks small.

## Local and international tips

- **Philippines:** patients often search with the district or mall name (“BGC”, “Ortigas”, “Alabang Town Center”) as well as the city. Include these naturally on location and treatment pages.
- **US, UK and Australia:** suburb and neighborhood searches matter, and insurance terms often appear (“accepts Medicare”, “NHS or private”).
- **Medical tourism:** if you treat international patients, research searches like “dental implants Philippines cost for foreigners” separately. They need different content about travel, accommodation and aftercare.

## Common mistakes

- Chasing broad health terms dominated by large hospitals and medical encyclopedias
- Creating separate thin pages for every keyword variation
- Ignoring the words patients use in favor of clinical terminology
- Forgetting to revisit research every six to twelve months as searches change

## Turning research into a plan

Your output should be a short list of **priority treatment pages**, each with a primary keyword group, and a handful of **supporting article topics** that link to it. That plan becomes your content roadmap for the next six months.
