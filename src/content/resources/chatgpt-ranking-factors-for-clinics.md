Patients are asking ChatGPT questions like “Which clinic in BGC is best for a full health check-up?” and acting on the answer. There’s no official list of ChatGPT ranking factors, but after testing hundreds of healthcare prompts and comparing which clinics get named, a clear pattern emerges.

Here are the six factors that matter most, and what to do about each.

## 1. Entity authority

ChatGPT is more likely to recommend a clinic that the wider web clearly knows about. Every credible mention of your clinic, whether in the news, on a medical society site, in a hospital directory or on a podcast, adds to that recognition.

**What to do:**

- Make sure every doctor is listed on the relevant medical society and hospital affiliation pages, with a link to your clinic.
- Offer your doctors as expert sources to local newspapers, radio and health websites.
- Get listed on the healthcare directories patients actually use in your market.
- Look for genuine community mentions on forums and Facebook groups. You can’t fake these, but you can earn them with great care and by being helpful in public.

## 2. Consistency

If your clinic’s name, address, phone number or services differ between Google, Facebook and directories, AI tools have to guess which version is right. Often they choose not to mention you at all.

**What to do:**

- List every profile that represents your clinic and every doctor.
- Standardize the clinic name (no stray “Inc.” or abbreviations), address format, phone number, opening hours and service list.
- Remove or merge duplicate listings, a common problem for clinics that have moved or rebranded.

## 3. Reviews and reputation

Google weighs its own reviews most heavily for Maps rankings. ChatGPT reads more broadly. Ratings and comments on Facebook, booking platforms and healthcare review sites all shape how it describes you.

**What to do:**

- Build a simple review request into your patient journey, such as a WhatsApp or SMS message after the visit.
- Prioritize Google, then choose one or two additional platforms that matter in your country.
- Respond to every review, including negative ones, calmly and without revealing any patient details.

## 4. Content as proof of expertise

Fewer patients read long blog posts than they used to, but content still matters because it is how AI tools judge your expertise. A clinic with clear, doctor-reviewed pages on every treatment it offers looks more authoritative than one with a single “Services” page.

**What to do:**

- Create one strong page per core treatment or condition you want patients for.
- Include who it’s for, how it works, risks, recovery, costs and FAQs.
- Show the reviewing doctor’s name and credentials on each page.

## 5. Freshness

AI tools favor information that looks current, especially for health topics where guidelines change. Pages that haven’t been touched in years, or that mention a doctor who left, signal neglect.

**What to do:**

- Put your core pages on a quarterly review schedule.
- Show a visible “Medically reviewed on” date and update it when you genuinely review the page.
- Check that links to outside sources such as health departments and studies still work.

## 6. Citations: the result of the other five

Being named in ChatGPT’s answers is the outcome, not a separate tactic. Every review, media mention, updated page and consistent listing contributes to it. That’s why GEO works best as an ongoing program rather than a one-off project.

## How the factors work together

| Factor | Main lever | Owner at the clinic |
|---|---|---|
| Entity authority | PR, associations, directories | Clinic manager + doctors |
| Consistency | Listing audit and cleanup | Marketing partner |
| Reviews | Post-visit review requests | Front desk |
| Content | Treatment and FAQ pages | Marketing partner + reviewing doctor |
| Freshness | Quarterly content reviews | Marketing partner |
| Citations | Result of all of the above | Everyone |

## Where to start

If you only do three things this quarter, do these:

1. **Audit and fix your listings** so every profile says the same thing.
2. **Rebuild your top three treatment pages** with clear answers, FAQs and a named medical reviewer.
3. **Start a steady review program** across Google and one other platform.
